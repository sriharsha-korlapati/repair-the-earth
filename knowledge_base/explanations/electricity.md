## Why does household electricity cause carbon emissions in India?

Electricity used in Indian households largely comes from the national power grid.
A significant portion of this grid is powered by fossil fuels, especially coal.

Coal-based power plants emit large amounts of carbon dioxide (CO₂) when generating electricity.

## How electricity consumption translates to emissions

Electricity consumption is measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh).
Each unit of electricity consumed from the Indian grid emits carbon dioxide.

On average, the Indian electricity grid emits approximately 0.7 kg of CO₂ per kWh.
This value can vary slightly by region and time, but it is a widely used planning average.

## Why household electricity adds up quickly

Even common household appliances such as:
- Fans
- Refrigerators
- Lights
- Washing machines
- Air conditioners

consume electricity daily. Over a month, this adds up to significant energy use and emissions.

## How households can reduce electricity-related emissions

Households can reduce their carbon footprint by:
- Using energy-efficient appliances
- Switching off unused devices
- Improving natural ventilation and lighting
- Installing rooftop solar where possible
- Reducing air conditioner usage

Reducing electricity consumption directly reduces carbon emissions.
